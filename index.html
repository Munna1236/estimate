📐 How It's Calculated ▼

Brick Calculation:

Bricks = (Built-up Area × Wall-to-Floor Ratio × Bricks per sq ft of wall) + Wastage%
Thumb rule: ~15-18 bricks/sq ft for 9-inch wall; ~7-9 for 4.5-inch wall

Concrete Materials (M20: 1:1.5:3):

Slab Volume = Built-up Area × Slab Thickness (mm→m)
Beam/Column Volume ≈ 15-20% of slab volume (default adder)
Total Wet Concrete = Slab + Beams/Columns
Cement = (Total Volume / 5.5) bags; Sand = (Volume × 1.5/4.5) m³; Agg = (Volume × 3/4.5) m³
Water (w/c ratio = 0.5): Cement Qty (kg) × 0.5 Litres

Steel Estimation:

Mode 1 (Per sq ft): Steel = Built-up Area × Rate (kg/sq ft) × Wastage%
Mode 2 (Per m³): Steel = Concrete Volume × Rates by member type × Wastage%

🏗️ About Type 2 Buildings (RCC Framed Structures)

Definition & Characteristics

Type 2 buildings are reinforced concrete (RCC) framed structures—the most prevalent construction type in modern India. The structural system consists of a concrete skeleton (beams, columns, slabs) with masonry infill walls, combining strength, flexibility, and economy.

Structural System

  • Load Path: Vertical loads from slabs → beams → columns → footings → soil
  • Infill Walls: Typically 9-inch (230 mm) brick masonry or block walls between columns
  • Connection: Monolithic (continuous) concrete frame with no expansion joints
  • Floor Systems: One-way or two-way RCC slabs (100–150 mm thick)

Advantages

  • ✓ High structural strength and stiffness
  • ✓ Good fire and weather resistance
  • ✓ Flexible interior layouts with fewer columns
  • ✓ Excellent for multi-story residential and commercial use
  • ✓ Long design life (50–100 years)
  • ✓ Moderate construction cost with good quality assurance

Applications

  • • Residential complexes (apartments, villas)
  • • Office and commercial buildings
  • • Schools, colleges, and institutional buildings
  • • Shopping malls and retail centers
  • • Hospitals and healthcare facilities
  • • Hotels and hospitality structures

Design Life & Durability

Type 2 structures are designed for a service life of 50–100 years with proper maintenance. Durability depends on concrete grade, cover thickness, reinforcement quality, and environmental conditions. Coastal areas may require higher concrete grades (M25–M30) for salt resistance.

Material Standards (India)

  • • Concrete Grade: M20, M25, M30 (typical for RCC)
  • • Steel Reinforcement: TMT Fe500 or Fe500D (high-strength deformed bars)
  • • Bricks: Class A or B quality, 230×110×75 mm or 190×90×90 mm (modular)
  • • Cement: OPC 43-grade or PPC (Portland Pozzolana Cement)
  • • Sand: Machine-sand or river-sand, fineness modulus 2.75–3.00
  • • Aggregate: 20 mm nominal size, well-graded

Regulatory Compliance (India)

Type 2 buildings must comply with Indian Standards (IS Codes):

  • • IS 456:2000 – Code of Practice for Plain and Reinforced Concrete
  • • IS 1893:2016 – Earthquake Resistant Design and Construction
  • • IS 3956:2016 – Code of Practice for Brickwork and Blockwork
  • • NBC (National Building Code) – Fire safety, accessibility, ventilation
  • • Local Building Bye-laws – Floor space index (FSI), setbacks, height restrictions
